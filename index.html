The Science of Prevention
12 Risk Factors You Can Actually Change

The Lancet Commission identified 12 modifiable risk factors accounting for around 40% of worldwide dementias. Addressing them could prevent or delay millions of cases.

"There is sufficiently strong evidence that modifying these 12 risk factors could prevent or delay a significant proportion of dementia cases worldwide."

— The Lancet Commission on Dementia Prevention, 2024
🎓

Low Education

Less formal education in early life reduces cognitive reserve, making the brain more vulnerable to dementia-related changes later on.

Early Life
👂

Hearing Loss

Untreated mid-life hearing loss is the single largest modifiable risk factor. Hearing aids can significantly reduce dementia risk.

Mid Life
🫀

High Blood Pressure

Hypertension in midlife damages brain blood vessels. Managing it is one of the most impactful preventive steps you can take.

Mid Life
🚬

Smoking

Smokers have a significantly higher risk of dementia. Quitting at any age reduces risk — with brain benefits beginning almost immediately.

Mid Life
⚖️

Obesity

Being overweight in midlife raises dementia risk through links to diabetes, hypertension, and inflammation — all of which affect brain health.

Mid Life
🍺

Excessive Alcohol

Drinking more than 21 units per week is associated with accelerated brain ageing and higher dementia risk. Reducing intake makes a real difference.

Mid Life
🧪

Diabetes

Type 2 diabetes is associated with up to double the risk of Alzheimer's disease. Blood sugar management is a key brain-protective strategy.

Later Life
😔

Depression

Both a risk factor and an early symptom, depression is linked to accelerated cognitive decline. Treatment supports long-term brain health.

Later Life
🏃

Physical Inactivity

Regular aerobic exercise increases brain blood flow, promotes neuroplasticity, and reduces multiple dementia risk factors simultaneously.

Later Life
🌫️

Air Pollution

Exposure to particulate matter and traffic pollution is linked to accelerated cognitive decline and higher dementia incidence in older adults.

Later Life
🏠

Social Isolation

Loneliness and lack of social engagement are strong risk factors. Meaningful relationships are as vital as physical health habits.

Later Life
🤕

Head Injury

Traumatic brain injury — especially repeated concussions — increases dementia risk. Wearing helmets and preventing falls are key measures.

Later Life
Prevention Strategies
What You Can Do Starting Today

Small, consistent lifestyle changes accumulate into meaningful brain protection over time. Here's where to start.

1

Move Your Body Daily

Even 150 minutes of moderate aerobic activity per week — brisk walking, swimming, cycling — measurably lowers your dementia risk and improves cognitive reserve.
2

Adopt the MIND Diet

A combination of the Mediterranean and DASH diets, the MIND diet emphasises leafy greens, berries, nuts, fish, and olive oil — shown to slow cognitive decline.
3

Prioritise Quality Sleep

During deep sleep, the brain clears amyloid plaques — a hallmark of Alzheimer's. Aim for 7–9 hours and treat sleep apnea if diagnosed.
4

Stay Socially & Mentally Active

Learning new skills, engaging socially, and challenging your brain with complex activities builds cognitive reserve that delays dementia symptom onset.
5

Manage Your Health Numbers

Know your blood pressure, blood sugar, and cholesterol. Working with your GP to keep them in healthy ranges directly protects brain vasculature.

SAGE Test

The Self-Administered Gerocognitive Exam (SAGE) is a brief, validated cognitive screening instrument designed to detect mild cognitive impairment (MCI) and early dementia. It evaluates memory, language, problem-solving, and visual-spatial skills. It can be completed independently in about 15 minutes with just a pen and paper.

⏱ 10–15 minutes ✏️ Pen & paper 🌍 10+ languages 📊 ~79% sensitivity
Download Free PDF →

⚠️ Free for personal and non-commercial educational use. Take completed test to your GP for scoring and interpretation. Results must be reviewed by a healthcare professional.

Alzheimer's Foundation of America + MoCA Cognition

MoCA XpressO™

A brief online version of the Montreal Cognitive Assessment (MoCA) — one of the world's most widely used cognitive screening tools. The MoCA XpressO™ is self-administered, takes 5–7 minutes, and is freely available through the Alzheimer's Foundation of America's partnership program. No insurance or minimum age required.

⏱ 5–7 minutes 💻 Online 🌍 36+ languages 📊 90%+ sensitivity
Take Free Online Test →

⚠️ Provided by the Alzheimer's Foundation of America. Discuss your score with your doctor. Not a diagnosis — a score suggesting impairment warrants further professional evaluation.

Mini-Cog© — Soo Borson, University of Washington

Mini-Cog©

A fast, simple 3-minute screening tool that combines a brief memory recall task and a clock-drawing test. Widely used in GP and primary care settings. Validated for detecting dementia across diverse populations and education levels. Printable and freely available for clinical and non-commercial use.

⏱ 3 minutes ✏️ Pen & paper 👩‍⚕️ GP-friendly

Psychological Assessment Resources (PAR)

MMSE (Mini-Mental State Exam)

The Mini-Mental State Examination is one of the oldest and most widely used cognitive screening tools, assessing orientation, memory, attention, language, and motor skills across 30 questions. It requires administration by a trained clinician and is best suited to measuring how far dementia has progressed rather than detecting early stages.

⏱ 10–15 minutes 👩‍⚕️ Clinician administered 📊 71% sensitivity
